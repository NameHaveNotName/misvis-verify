# MisVis Verify — Paper Framework

**Working title:** Beyond the AI Label: How Provenance Cues and Evidence-Grounded Visual Verification Shape Trust in Misleading Visualizations

**Date:** 2026-08-15

---

## 1. One-sentence contribution

We show that disclosing AI involvement in data visualizations can trigger indiscriminate skepticism, and that a structured, evidence-based visual-verification procedure can shift viewers from source-heuristic reasoning to chart-evidence reasoning, improving trust calibration and transferring to unseen charts.

---

## 2. Research gap and motivation

### 2.1 Practical problem

Generative AI tools can now produce charts, infographics, and data summaries at scale. Policymakers and platforms are therefore considering provenance labels (e.g., C2PA, "AI-generated" watermarks) to alert viewers. However, it is unclear whether such labels help people distinguish accurate from misleading visualizations or simply make them distrust all charts.

### 2.2 Theoretical problem

The misinformation literature shows that source cues often act as heuristics: people substitute "who made this?" for "is this accurate?". For data visualizations, this is especially problematic because charts contain internally checkable evidence—axes, values, titles, intervals—that viewers could use if prompted. Existing work on AI labels has focused on text and photorealistic images; controlled data visualizations have received less attention.

### 2.3 Research opportunity

If viewers can be taught to verify visual evidence rather than rely on source labels, we may achieve *trust calibration*: maintaining trust in accurate charts while reducing trust in misleading ones. This is a different goal than maximizing skepticism.

---

## 3. Research questions

**RQ1.** How does AI provenance disclosure affect users' trust and discernment between accurate and misleading visualizations?

**RQ2.** Can evidence-grounded visual verification (EGVV) reduce provenance-driven heuristic judgments and improve trust calibration?

**RQ3.** Do the effects of verification transfer to unseen visualizations after assistance is removed?

---

## 4. Hypotheses

| ID | Hypothesis | Rationale |
|----|------------|-----------|
| H1 | AI provenance cues reduce overall trust. | Source cues for AI-generated content are often interpreted negatively. |
| H2 | AI provenance cues do **not** necessarily increase trust discernment; they may produce general skepticism. | Without evidence prompts, viewers may discount both accurate and misleading charts. |
| H3 | EGVV increases trust discernment (Trust_Accurate − Trust_Misleading). | Teaching viewers to inspect axes, values, and encodings should selectively reduce trust only in misleading charts. |
| H4 | EGVV improves detection of misleading charts in a transfer phase without provenance cues or assistance. | Learning a reusable verification procedure should generalize to new data contexts. |

---

## 5. Theoretical framing

### 5.1 Heuristic-Systematic Model

- **Provenance cue → heuristic processing.** Viewers use the AI label as a simple rejection rule.
- **EGVV → systematic processing.** Viewers are prompted to inspect specific visual evidence.

### 5.2 Trust calibration

Appropriate trust means trust matches ground truth:
- High trust in accurate charts.
- Low trust in misleading charts.

Indiscriminate skepticism (low trust in both) is a calibration failure.

### 5.3 Inoculation / accuracy nudge

EGVV functions as a domain-specific accuracy nudge: it shifts attention from "who made this?" to "what does the evidence show?".

## 6. Method overview

### 6.1 Design

Mixed design:
- **Factor A: Provenance** (within-subjects): AI-assisted vs. no provenance.
- **Factor B: Chart integrity** (within-subjects): Accurate vs. misleading.
- **Factor C: Intervention** (between-subjects): Control vs. EGVV.

### 6.2 Stimuli

- 6 mechanisms × 2 contexts × 2 integrity versions = 24 SVGs = 12 matched pairs.
- Mechanisms: truncated axis, cherry-picked time range, hidden uncertainty, area encoding distortion, arbitrary color emphasis, misleading title.
- All SVGs generated by a Python script to ensure visual consistency.
- Stimulus IDs are neutral (S001, S002, …) to prevent answer leakage via filenames.

### 6.3 Procedure

1. Informed consent.
2. Participant setup (random ID, condition assignment).
3. Baseline: 4 trials (2 accurate, 2 misleading), no provenance, no intervention.
4. Main phase: 12 trials (counterbalanced for integrity × provenance).
   - Initial judgment (trust, misleading judgment, confidence).
   - Intervention: control re-inspection or EGVV (Locate → Explain → Verify → Compare).
   - Post judgment (trust, misleading judgment, confidence).
5. Transfer: 6 trials (3 accurate, 3 misleading), no provenance, no intervention.
6. Post-study questionnaire.
7. Completion and local JSON export.

### 6.4 Participants

Pilot: N = 24–30.
Planned main study: N ≈ 160–240 (to be determined after pilot effect-size estimation).
Recruitment: online platform or university participant pool.

---

## 7. Key dependent variables

### 7.1 Trust

0–100 slider after each chart.

### 7.2 Misleading judgment

Yes / No / Unsure.

### 7.3 Confidence

0–100 slider.

### 7.4 Response time

Time from stimulus onset to judgment submission.

### 7.5 Derived metrics

- **Trust discernment:** mean trust(accurate) − mean trust(misleading).
- **Detection accuracy:** proportion of correct misleading/accurate judgments.
- **False positive rate:** accurate charts judged misleading.
- **False negative rate:** misleading charts judged not misleading.
- **Belief revision:** trust_post − trust_pre.

---

## 8. Planned analyses

### 8.1 Detection accuracy

Mixed-effects logistic regression:

```r
Correct ~ Provenance * Integrity * Intervention +
          (1 | Participant) + (1 | Item)
```

### 8.2 Trust

Mixed-effects linear regression:

```r
Trust ~ Provenance * Integrity * Intervention +
        (1 | Participant) + (1 | Item)
```

### 8.3 Transfer

Separate model restricted to transfer trials:

```r
Correct ~ Integrity * Intervention + (1 | Participant) + (1 | Item)
Trust ~ Integrity * Intervention + (1 | Participant) + (1 | Item)
```

### 8.4 Belief revision

Within-trial change model:

```r
Trust_post - Trust_pre ~ Integrity * Intervention + (1 | Participant)
```

## 9. Expected results pattern

If hypotheses hold:

| Condition | Accurate trust | Misleading trust | Discernment | Transfer detection |
|-----------|---------------|------------------|-------------|-------------------|
| Control, no provenance | moderate | moderate-low | low-moderate | baseline |
| Control, AI provenance | lower | lower | no gain / slight loss | baseline |
| EGVV, no provenance | moderate | lower | higher | improved |
| EGVV, AI provenance | moderate-high | lower | highest | improved |

Key interpretation: EGVV should protect accurate-chart trust while reducing misleading-chart trust.

## 12. Limitations to acknowledge

1. **Stimuli are synthetic.** Although grounded in real misleader taxonomy, they are not real news graphics.
2. **Online convenience sample.** Generalization to broader populations is limited.
3. **Single-session training.** EGVV is brief; long-term retention is not tested.
4. **Self-reported strategy.** The post-study item may not capture actual cognitive strategies.
5. **WEIRD bias.** Most participants likely from Western, educated, industrialized, rich, democratic backgrounds.
